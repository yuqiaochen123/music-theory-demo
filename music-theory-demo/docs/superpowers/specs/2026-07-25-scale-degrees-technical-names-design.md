# Scale Degrees and Technical Names Design

## Goal

Add a complete Grade 5 lesson and ten-question practice session for scale degrees and technical names in major and minor keys.

## Lesson structure

The lesson has four cards in the established two-column format.

1. C major: all seven degree names.
2. A natural minor: all seven degree names, with degree 7 named subtonic.
3. A harmonic minor: raised degree 7 named leading note.
4. A melodic minor: raised ascending degree 7 named leading note and natural descending degree 7 named subtonic.

Every card shows a complete engraved scale with visible degree-name labels, separate controls for the tonic and target degree, and a complete-scale control. The existing responsive two-column card layout remains in force.

## Technical-name rules

- Degree 1: tonic
- Degree 2: supertonic
- Degree 3: mediant
- Degree 4: subdominant
- Degree 5: dominant
- Degree 6: submediant
- Degree 7: leading note when one semitone below tonic; subtonic when a whole tone below tonic

## Practice

Ten exercises show one written degree in a full scale and ask for its technical name. The bank includes major, natural minor, harmonic minor, and melodic-minor ascending/descent contexts, with both leading-note and subtonic answers.

## Acceptance criteria

- Grade 5 module 09 becomes available and links to `topic.html?topic=scale-degrees`.
- The lesson renders all four labelled scale cards with matching MIDI playback.
- Degree 7 is correctly named as leading note or subtonic according to its pitch context.
- Practice contains ten valid, varied degree-name questions.
- Desktop controls stay aligned and narrow screens do not overflow.
