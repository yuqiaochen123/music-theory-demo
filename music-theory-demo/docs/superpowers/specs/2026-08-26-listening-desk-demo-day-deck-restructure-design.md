# Listening Desk Demo Day Deck Restructure

## Purpose

Condense the current 18-slide Chinese Demo Day presentation into 12 main slides plus 2 backup slides. The revised deck must make the user need and product value clearer at the beginning, keep JTBD and Four Forces central, and explain Kano through implemented product decisions rather than abstract theory.

## Audience and outcome

The audience is the Demo Day judging panel. By the end, judges should understand that Listening Desk solves a specific ABRSM music-theory failure moment: students can recognize notation on paper but cannot reliably hear or compare what it means. They should see a traceable path from evidence to product decisions, experience one complete task flow, and understand the conditions under which the product should continue or pivot.

## Deliverables

- One revised PPTX with 12 main slides and 2 backup slides.
- Preserve the existing cream, wine, berry, pink, gold and green visual language.
- Preserve source notes and speaker notes on every slide.
- Keep the current cover design with the Listening Desk symbol and blindfolded metaphor.
- Do not modify the website.
- Keep the current uploaded Google Slides copy unchanged unless the user explicitly asks for a native Slides update.

## Main slide sequence

1. **Cover** — “Doing music theory without sound is like drawing blindfolded.” Keep the app symbol and the restrained “See it. Hear it. Understand it.” treatment.
2. **User, failure moment and context** — identify 14–18-year-old ABRSM learners; show the book image; explain that books provide structure, definitions and exercises but cannot provide immediate listening validation. Add the strongest research signals: 5/6 encounter the notation-to-sound gap, immediate playback helpfulness 4.2/5, and usage intention 8.2/10.
3. **Evidence of demand and added value** — combine survey priorities, current workarounds and concise interview evidence. Show that users skip, search YouTube or find an instrument when stuck. State the careful conclusion: users do not necessarily abandon books; they abandon the book-only workflow at the failure moment.
4. **Concise JTBD and product promise** — use exactly:
   - When: 当我看懂 Grade 5 乐谱，却听不出差别时
   - I want: 我想立即试听并比较
   - So I can: 从而理解并记住概念
   Center headings and statements horizontally and vertically inside the three cards. Increase internal spacing so no text appears close to card edges.
5. **Four Forces today** — retain Push, Pull, Anxiety and Habit with current evidence and strengths. Clarify that static scores describe the present and are not the final Go/No-Go decision.
6. **Four Forces trend decision** — retain the future-tendency logic: continue only if Push + Pull grow faster than Anxiety + Habit. Vertically center the GO and NO-GO/PIVOT titles and explanations with generous padding. Keep the judgment conditional and evidence-seeking.
7. **Product solution and rationale** — explain the product through four layers: trust, understanding, validation and continuity. Tie actual implemented features to the JTBD rather than listing pages.
8. **User journey / story map** — preserve the failure-moment starting point and one horizontal path: get stuck → locate concept → see notation → listen/compare → short practice → reasoned feedback → saved review.
9. **Core demonstration** — show one Grade 5 interval scenario from selection through listening, practice, feedback, retry and saved next step.
10. **Key differentiator** — emphasize the uninterrupted loop of notation + sound + active answer + explanation + retry. Explain why the value is the complete feedback loop, not merely the audio player.
11. **Kano product decisions** — combine Must-be, Performance, Attractive, Indifferent and Reverse logic on one main slide. Include the context-sensitive rationale for Streak and AI: encouragement or timely help can be attractive, while guilt, interruption or answer replacement can become reverse attributes.
12. **Evaluation and next validation** — successful aspects, current limitations and future tests. End with the conditional GO criterion and observable learning outcomes.

## Backup slides

13. **Research detail** — full survey counts, difficult topics, interview quotes and sample limitations.
14. **Feature-level Kano matrix** — detailed classification of implemented, partial and intentionally avoided features.

## Visual and layout requirements

- Keep 16:9 dimensions and existing brand palette.
- Use one primary claim per slide and avoid dense UI-like card grids where a simpler composition is possible.
- Center all text inside JTBD and GO/NO-GO cards both horizontally and vertically.
- Use adequate internal margins; text must not touch or visually crowd card edges.
- Preserve legible body text and shorten copy before reducing font size.
- Render and inspect every final slide. Correct clipping, wrapping, accidental overlap, inconsistent spacing and broken image crops.

## Source and accuracy requirements

- Retain the PDF report, course material, teacher Q&A and local-product references in slide notes.
- Do not claim the six-person exploratory sample proves market demand.
- Do not claim users will fully abandon books.
- Present Kano classifications as hypotheses derived from JTBD, interviews and product logic until validated with Kano questions.
- Present the Four Forces decision as a trend to validate through real use, not a static arithmetic conclusion.
